## Context

<p class="lede">Data revealed that over 90% of users were abandoning their carts, and only 9.7% progressed to checkout, a critical blocker to customer acquisition and revenue growth. While our checkout flow performed well, the cart experience was failing to build the trust and clarity needed to move users forward.</p>

### What's the problem? What's the goals?

<div class="cards">
  <div><h5>📈 Business Needs</h5><p>Increase revenue by improving the conversion funnel. Reduce cart abandonment and help more users complete their purchases.</p></div>
  <div><h5>❓ Problem to Solve</h5><p>The existing cart experience lacked clarity and trust-building elements., leading to a <strong>90.3% abandonment rate</strong>.</p></div>
  <div><h5>📍 Project Goal</h5><p>Redesign the shopping cart with a trust-focused, mobile-optimized layout that clearly communicates shipping, pricing, and available actions.</p></div>
  <div><h5>🪨 Challenge</h5><p>How can we redesign the cart experience under tight engineering resources while ensuring the new flow builds user confidence and supports different payment behaviors across devices?</p></div>
</div>

### Solutions

A streamlined cart experience focused on clarity, transparency, and guiding the user toward the next step.

<div class="cards three">
  <div><h5>Trust Enhancements</h5><ul><li>Added estimated shipping dates to reduce hesitation</li><li>Moved discount code input from checkout to cart to offer value earlier</li></ul></div>
  <div><h5>Price Transparency</h5><ul><li>Introduced a clear summary of total costs (including shipping)</li></ul></div>
  <div><h5>CTA Clarity</h5><ul><li>Separated the primary checkout CTA from quick-pay buttons</li><li>Improved visual hierarchy to increase action rates</li></ul></div>
</div>

### What I Learned From Unexpected Data Results & Next Steps

<div class="cards three">
  <div><h5>Why Checkout Completed Rate Increased More Than Checkout Rate</h5><p>This suggests the redesign helped reassure users during the checkout process, likely by improving clarity around final pricing, shipping details, and discounts, which reduced drop-off after checkout started.</p></div>
  <div><h5>Limited Impact on Reached Checkout Rate: Entry Friction Still Exists</h5><p>Users might still not see enough value or urgency to move forward. Overwhelming checkout options (like multiple quick-pay buttons) may still cause confusion.</p></div>
  <div><h5>Next Focus: Reduce Pre-Checkout Friction And Streamline Checkout</h5><p>Run session replays or click maps on the cart page to identify hesitation. A/B test simplifying quick-pay options (e.g., hide them behind a dropdown or reduce visual noise).</p></div>
</div>
